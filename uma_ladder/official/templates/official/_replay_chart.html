Runners sit at their real distance and lane, so you watch the pack form, split and close. Default camera fits the whole field (it never spreads much more than ~30 m, so nobody gets pushed off-screen); picking a runner pins them to the centre so you can watch your own battle instead of the leader's. 2. The timeline — the whole race at once: running position, phase bands, and each runner's real last-spurt point. Following a runner also marks every skill they fired, as a dot on their line with a bar for how long it lasted. 3. The commentary — every skill activation and race moment in order, lit as the clock reaches it. The chart shows shape; this says what actually happened. 4. Sectionals — average speed per 200 m against the field's mean for that same segment.
